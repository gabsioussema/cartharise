01 / VISION

Product foundation

Product mission

Help people understand their values, notice patterns, create an adaptable direction, and translate self-reflection into concrete, sustainable action with supportive guidance.

Promise

“Understand yourself. Choose your path. Rise with purpose.”

Positioning

A personal clarity-and-action companion—not therapy, not a diagnosis, not a rigid productivity tracker.

Problem statement

People encounter insightful long-form self-development content but struggle to understand abstract concepts, complete demanding exercises, connect insights, and follow through.

Desired outcome

Within the first guided reset, the user owns a meaningful first draft of a Life Compass and a feasible next step. Beyond that, they can observe, act, learn, and revise.

Realistic claim

One day can help create clarity; it cannot guarantee that someone's entire life will be fixed.

Product goals

  • Make every exercise understandable.
  • Turn insights into user-owned plans.
  • Encourage sustainable action.
  • Protect emotional agency and privacy.
  • Make returning feel useful, not obligatory.

Non-goals for MVP

  • Diagnose personality or mental health.
  • Promise overnight transformation.
  • Predict hidden motivations.
  • Build a social ranking network.
  • Replace human support or therapy.
  • Require paid AI to complete exercises.

Core differentiator

  • One question at a time.
  • Plain-language help + neutral examples.
  • Personal direction stays editable.
  • Connected morning → day → evening journey.
  • Daily actions, a 30-day reflection path and weekly learning loop.
02 / RESEARCH HYPOTHESES

Users & jobs to be done

These are initial personas and hypotheses to validate through discovery interviews, not confirmed market research.

PERSONA A

The overwhelmed starter

Wants change but doesn't know where to begin; feels discouraged by ambitious planning systems.

Job: “Help me make sense of my dissatisfaction without overwhelming me.”

Need: a calm, skippable first experience.

PERSONA B

The reflective but stuck learner

Consumes self-improvement content, journals, but rarely translates insight into action.

Job: “Help me turn my thoughts into one experiment I can try this week.”

Need: bridge from reflection to direction and tasks.

PERSONA C

The direction-seeking builder

Has goals and ability to act but feels misaligned with values or has difficulty prioritizing.

Job: “Give me a structure for choosing a mission without losing my wellbeing.”

Need: Compass, boundaries, monthly project, reviews.

Discovery interview prompts

  1. Tell us about the last time you tried to make a meaningful change. What happened after a week?
  2. When you read self-improvement material, which concepts or exercises remain confusing?
  3. How comfortable are you writing about personal worries in an app? What would make it feel safe?
  4. What would make reflection worth revisiting tomorrow, one week later, or one month later?
  5. What is your preferred format: typing, voice, short choices, or a combination?
  6. What kind of reminder would help, and which would feel intrusive?
03 / EXPERIENCE FLOW

The end-to-end journey

Discover→Choose pace→Learn→Morning mirror→Daytime interrupts→Evening synthesis→Life Compass→Daily quests + 30-day path→Weekly review↻
00—01

Acquisition & onboarding

Show meaningful promise, clear inspiration credit, and permission to proceed without reading the article. Offer optional name, accessibility choices, privacy explanation, and flexible vs one-day pacing. No account required for demo.

02—03

Education & reset

Introduce seven simplified concepts on demand; guide 14 morning prompts; six daytime awareness pauses with editable times; three optional contemplations; seven evening prompts. A user can skip and revisit any prompt.

04—05

Direction & action

Offer user-approved mapping from relevant evening answers into six Compass fields; let users edit everything. Turn daily levers into two or three tasks with no punitive streak.

06—07

Continuity & evolution

Open Today as the default return view; journal freely, complete small actions, revisit boundaries and goals, and use weekly reflections to adapt the plan. Offer private backup and erasure controls.

Main navigation should contain Today, Awakening, Reset, Compass, Missions, Journal, Evolution, Inspiration, Settings. These are 8 functional modules plus Settings, while onboarding is a separate entry flow. The earlier concept's Mirror, Vision, Interrupt, and Breakthrough appear as subflows inside the Reset; this avoids overcrowding the navigation.

07 / INTERACTION DESIGN

Behavior & interaction rules

Guided question UX

  1. Present one question with context, estimated effort optional, and progress.
  2. Explain unfamiliar terms in plain language on request.
  3. Provide a clearly labeled example, never a “correct answer.”
  4. Autosave on text input where storage is supported.
  5. Support Back, Skip, Jump, Save & Continue.
  6. Maintain the user’s wording; avoid rewriting their inner experience without consent.

Reflection-to-action mapping

  1. Evening #3 → anti-vision; #4 → vision.
  2. Evening #5 → one-year mission; #6 → one-month project.
  3. Evening #7 → daily levers.
  4. Constraints are explicitly entered by the user, not inferred.
  5. Use an opt-in “populate Compass” action and preserve existing edits.
  6. Parse daily levers as draft quests; users approve or change them.

Daytime notifications

Editable six times in local time. A production app needs user opt-in, permission prompts, time zone handling, snooze/quiet hours, and meaningful delivery when the app is closed.

HTML demo behavior: export a 30-day iCalendar file; import into the user's calendar. This is not browser push.

Resilience and autonomy

Allow pauses, skips, returning to prior answers, redoing the reset, changing goals, and deleting information. No streak shame, forced confessions, “life failure” ratings, or assumed psychological conclusions.

Key interface states to design

Loading & saving

Local autosave indicator; async save for production, with retry and offline queue.

Empty states

No compass yet, no quests, no journal entries, unfinished reset, no check-in response.

Interrupted sessions

Restore last phase/question. If local storage is blocked, disclose and support export.

Completion

Calm confirmation and next suggestion; no unearned “you fixed your life” celebration.

High emotion

Offer skip, soften prompt, pause, and signpost professional support if necessary.

Errors

Explicit messages for denied notifications, failed imports, unavailable sync, and invalid inputs.

Demo architecture — implemented

  • Standalone HTML + CSS + vanilla JavaScript.
  • Works offline after opening the file; no external libraries.
  • Browser localStorage for reflections, Compass, tasks, notes, reviews.
  • Downloadable JSON backup/import and calendar .ics export.
  • No account, API calls, server, analytics, or AI.

Production architecture — proposal

  • Responsive React/Next.js or equivalent with typed domain modules.
  • API server + relational DB such as PostgreSQL.
  • Authentication optional initially, required for cross-device sync.
  • Encrypt data in transit and at rest; consent-aware retention policy.
  • Scheduled notifications through platform/browser-supported service.
  • Server-side authorization on every private resource.
  • Feature flags and versioned prompt library.
Candidate REST endpoints: GET /journeys/current, PATCH /answers/:id, GET/PATCH /compass, GET/POST/PATCH /quests, GET/POST /journal, GET/POST /reviews, PATCH /reminder-preferences, POST /export, DELETE /account. Finalize only after authentication, consent, and data ownership model are decided.
09 / QUALITY BARS

Non-functional requirements & safeguards

Privacy & security

  • Default to private entries and data minimization.
  • Explain storage location clearly; no deceptive “encrypted private cloud” claim.
  • Encrypt production data in transit and at rest, with robust access control.
  • Support export, correction, and permanent deletion.
  • Confirm retention, lawful basis, processor agreements, and GDPR requirements before launch.
  • Never place journal contents in analytics, logs, or notification text by default.

Accessibility

  • Target WCAG 2.2 AA for production.
  • Keyboard navigation and visible focus indicators.
  • Semantic labels, accessible announcements, logical heading order.
  • Responsive from 320 px through desktop; avoid horizontal scrolling except dense tables.
  • Support reduced motion and screen readers.
  • Do not require color alone to communicate state.

Performance & reliability

  • Suggested production target: core content interactive under ~3s on a mid-range mobile connection, subject to testing.
  • Draft save feedback; retry on network failures.
  • Preserve inputs on navigation and interruptions.
  • Gracefully degrade offline; define sync conflicts if cross-device.
  • Versioned migrations and tested backup restore.

Emotional safety

  • Users may skip, pause, and choose softer wording.
  • No psychological diagnoses or confident motive extraction.
  • No self-hate framing, panic-inducing countdowns, or punitive gamification.
  • For potentially vulnerable users, avoid suggesting urgent drastic life decisions.
  • Define escalation copy for crisis disclosures if AI or community features launch.

Content integrity

  • Article references and credit visible and clickable.
  • Paraphrased exercises, independently authored explanations.
  • Distinguish source ideas from Cartharise additions.
  • Allow reviewed prompt revisions without breaking prior saved answers.
  • Do not claim the psychological frameworks are universally established diagnoses.

Localization & usability

  • Launch language: English; architecture ready for other locales.
  • Use local clock format and user timezone.
  • Dates should be readable; content writing at accessible reading level.
  • Provide flexible schedules, not a rigid “one day or failure” path.
  • Use minimal military olive / forest green + bronze without combat imagery.

11 / ACCEPTANCE & QA

Critical end-to-end tests

Happy-path scenarios

  1. First launch → onboarding → start reset → response saved.
  2. Answer 14 morning prompts; navigate back and verify unchanged text.
  3. Record a daytime check-in; return later and verify persisted answer.
  4. Answer seven evening prompts → populate Compass with confirmation.
  5. Edit Compass without overwriting custom text on later import.
  6. Convert daily levers to tasks; complete, reopen, and delete a task.
  7. Create/edit/delete journal entry; create/delete weekly review.
  8. Export JSON → clear local data → restore backup.
  9. Export .ics, import into a calendar, verify six recurring events.
  10. Open article link and verify author attribution.

Edge-case scenarios

  1. Skip all prompts; user can still navigate and return later.
  2. Very long answers and special characters are safely rendered.
  3. Local storage blocked or quota reached → honest notice.
  4. Malformed import file rejected without losing current data.
  5. Device with narrow screen and large font setting remains usable.
  6. Keyboard-only and screen-reader navigation.
  7. Switch app/tab mid-input; state persists without silent loss.
  8. Notification permission denied; alternative stays available.
  9. Change timezone, locale, or daylight saving rules.
  10. Repeated reset preserves the desired historical state in production.

Definition of done for MVP

  • All source segments are mapped to tested, independently written in-app activities.
  • Core flows work on supported mobile and desktop browsers with screen readers and keyboard.
  • Privacy notice, export, delete, and source credit are complete and reviewed.
  • Real reminders are opt-in and time-zone aware; no false notification claims.
  • Security test coverage includes authorization, injection, data leaks, and backup recovery.
  • Content has been reviewed for emotional tone, accessibility, and potential harm.
  • Users can stop at any phase and return without losing their writing.
  • App communicates the distinction between self-development and clinical care.

Linking to the article and crediting its author is appropriate. Credit does not itself grant permission to republish substantial original text, exclusive artwork, or a closely copied commercial workbook. Obtain a legal review and, where needed, permission before a public release.

Scope limitation

This is a detailed elicitation draft and an interactive local demo. Requirements marked “proposed” are not validated facts. Live push, authentication, synchronization, protected backend storage, production analytics, and AI are not built into the companion demo.
